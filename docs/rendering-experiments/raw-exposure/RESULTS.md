# RAW exposure experiment: initial results

**Historical results and recommendation.** This experiment predates the implemented RAW exposure
routing and the corrections → Look → finishing order. Its recommendation below records the decision
at the time, not current product behavior. See [the current render order](../../LOOKS.md#render-order)
and [RAW exposure routing](../../ENGINEERING_GUIDE.md#raw-exposure-routing).

Run on 4 November 2026, macOS 27.2, Apple Silicon. Primary fixtures: `realworldtest/DSC00324.ARW` (6000 × 4000) and `DSC01153.ARW` (9504 × 6336); the requested `DSC01104.ARW` was not available. Two DNGs were also tested as supplemental controls. The ARWs were decoded at an 800-pixel maximum dimension; this is an initial screen-scale test, not a full-resolution quality study. Original files are not included. JSON captures each setting and measurement; see [setup and limitations](README.md). The two supplemental DNG JSONs predate the addition of matched-display gains and have unnormalized contact sheets.

## Findings

**Recommendation: keep the current production exposure path for now; investigate a higher-headroom decoder output and a versioned RAW exposure prototype before choosing either.** The ARWs show a substantial decoder-dependent difference, but these tests do not yet prove that either path preserves more *recoverable* scene detail at matched brightness. An unqualified switch would also change the relationship between exposure, Looks and saved edits. These results are insufficient to establish the requested success criterion or a general photographic-quality advantage.

At EDR 0 and EV −2, A versus B differs across nearly every finite RGB channel: RMS linear-channel difference is 0.0239 for DSC00324 and 0.0176 for DSC01153; at 0 EV A and B match exactly. These differences demonstrate different tone/color responses, **not** recovered highlights. Their median linear luminances are respectively 0.0773 vs 0.0480, and 0.0210 vs 0.0132. At +2 EV the fraction with any channel ≥1 was 66.71% versus 4.90% for the first ARW and 47.50% versus 11.50% for the second, but these are unequal-brightness, scene-wide measurements and cannot establish clipping at the sensor or superior highlight texture. The paired float differences and per-channel statistics are in the JSON. Boost 0 produces visibly darker neutral output: for DSC00324 its median is 0.1461 versus 0.3090 with default boost; for DSC01153, 0.0525 versus 0.0839. It changes the tonal rendering, and no conclusion that it recovers more information follows from those numbers.

The 0 EV and ±2 EV contact sheets apply a recorded **single global median-matching gain** to each panel; examine the unmodified `A/B/C/D` PNGs as well. Even a matched median does not align local highlights or tonal curves. No manually selected white-cloth, sky, saturated-highlight, edge or deep-shadow ROI was evaluated, and no independent sensor ground truth or blind viewing study was performed. Thus whether differences remain meaningful at normal viewing size, whether bright-region details are genuinely recovered, and how color or noise behave at equivalent local brightness remain open. The two Sony files show the phenomenon generalizes to these two files, not to all ARW cameras or scenes. The DNG controls yielded exactly identical A/B and C/D float arrays for this sampled graph on this OS, demonstrating that the behavior is decoder/file-dependent rather than a general rule.

Changing EDR from 0 to 1 changes measured output, including values above 1; it is not a controlled proof that RGB exposure with EDR can match a RAW-exposure recovery. Neither EDR nor boost should be changed globally without a matched-scene, fixed-region study. RGB and RAW exposure also respond differently at the same EV, so routing only RAW through the decoder risks inconsistent RAW/JPEG slider behavior. Local masks remain RGB and cannot selectively alter RAW development.

All inputs were left at their decoder's own WB, baseline, recovery, lens and detail defaults, recorded in each JSON. On these ARWs highlight recovery and gamut mapping were enabled and lens correction supported/enabled. Note a significant empirical qualification to the prior inspection: **DSC00324 reports supported versions 6, 7, 8 and 9 but the fresh filter reported version 8**; DSC01153 reports 7 and 8 and selects 8. The installed header says new filters default to the newest available version, but this observation means the supported list alone cannot prove which version is selected in practice. No version was explicitly selected and no version-9 resources were downloaded. Recheck on the target OS and file, and do not assume the newest listed version is active.

## Performance

For DSC00324 the isolated 800-pixel proxy measured first construct+render 140 ms, fresh RAW exposure ticks 102–113 ms, and RGB exposure on a materialized float source 0.9–7.8 ms. One full-resolution RAW −1 EV construct+render took 253 ms; its process peak RSS was approximately 1.21 GiB. These are single-run, CPU-readback-inclusive numbers without confidence intervals and are not real UI time-to-first/settled or a controlled GPU/CPU utilization profile. The all-modes measurement process peaked at 3.31 GiB on DSC00324 and 6.47 GiB on DSC01153, including repeatedly rendered PNGs and Core Image resources; this cannot be assigned to one frame or interpreted as app memory.

The app's developed-source keys include RAW settings, so changing decoder exposure invalidates source and downstream prefix reuse; RGB exposure can retain the developed source. The existing interactive RAW session can reuse a filter, which this cold-filter proxy does not model. A real `RenderEngine` benchmark is needed before judging interaction practicality; neither settled-frame latency nor actual cache invalidations or CPU/GPU utilization were directly measured here.

## Next measurements

1. Select fixed, high-resolution ROIs on additional overexposed Sony ARWs (clouds, white fabric, saturated colored lights, edges and shadow patches). Render full-resolution float buffers. Compare spatially corresponding pixel pairs and luminance/chromaticity ranks after an explicitly recorded midtone alignment; use local contrast, texture and repeated-exposure or bracketed reference to distinguish true recovered structure from nonlinear tone mapping.
2. Compare EDR 0/1 and boost 0/1 under identical matched viewing transforms, especially RGB EV with EDR versus RAW EV. Record highlight recovery and all decoder settings. Check colors and shadow noise in matched fixed ROIs, not whole-image variance.
3. Benchmark app `RenderEngine` with its reusable interactive session and cache counters, repeated p50/p95, memory and full export. Preserve existing document semantics and test Looks on/off before considering a versioned routing change.

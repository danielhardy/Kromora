## Objective

Opening a photo with a long edit history must not delay the histogram or the confirmed frame. The stored edit document load has to cost the same for a 134-revision photo as for a 20-revision one.

## Symptom

Navigating to DSC00981.ARW (134 revisions) and DSC01279.ARW (164) showed the histogram 1–3 s after the image, while DSC01019.ARW (20 revisions) updated in under 1 s. The image itself appeared as fast as any other, because the stored frame is presented first. The histogram describes the confirmed frame, so it waits for `confirmed`.

## Diagnosis

1. **Not masks.** The first theory, semantic masks, was wrong for DSC00981. Its current revision has no local adjustments, and resetting it did not help.
2. **Not the histogram computation.** The Release `StoredEditAdoptionBenchmark` gave DSC00981 and DSC01019 identical timings (histogram after ready under 5 ms) when both had stored edits.
3. **The wait is the stored document load.** The `photo-switch` log (`log stream --predicate 'subsystem == "com.kromora.app"'`) shows `confirmed` firing at the same instant as `storedDocumentLoaded`, which took 1.7–2.6 s for DSC00981 and about 0.2 s for DSC01019.
4. **Cause.** Reading a photo's `asset.json` validated the whole history. `PortableLibraryPackage.validate(record)` called `packageURL(for:)` for every revision pointer and every XMP pointer, and `PackagePath.url(in:)` resolves symlinks for the root and for every path component. That is roughly 2,000 symlink resolutions per read for 134 revisions. A single `EditDocumentStore.load` then read and parsed the record three times (`load`, then `editPointer` twice inside `readEditSidecar`). Cost grew with history length, and Debug builds, which `swift run` uses, made it worse.

## Changes

- `PortableLibraryPackage.validate(record)`: revision and XMP pointers are now checked with `PackagePath.isLexicallySafe`. The symlink-containment check is unchanged where it matters, since every pointer file is opened through `packageURL(for:)` in `readEditRevision` and `readEditSidecar`.
- `PortablePackageEditSidecar`: `readEditRevision`, `readEditSidecar` and `editPointer` take an optional already-parsed `PortablePackageAssetRecord`. `readEditHistory` passes its record through instead of re-parsing it once per revision, which was O(n²) in the history length.
- `EditDocumentStore.load` passes the record it already read into `readEditSidecar`, so a load parses `asset.json` once.

## Evidence

Read-only timing against the real library, Release build, package read (`readEditSidecar`):

| Photo | Revisions | Before | After the validate change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| DSC00981 | 134 | 24 ms | 3.5 ms |
| DSC01279 | 164 | 30 ms | 5 ms |
| DSC01019 | 20 | 4 ms | 1 ms |

Debug app (`swift run`), alternating DSC00981 and DSC01019, `confirmed` after selection:

| Stage | DSC00981 | DSC01019 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Before | 1.7–2.6 s | about 0.2 s |
| After the validate change | about 0.75 s | about 0.2 s |
| After the single parse | 147–198 ms | 175–215 ms |

## Verification

- Package, path, edit-store, validation and restore tests pass (49 tests), including the symlink and path-escape cases.
- Not run: the full serial and fast lanes; the display-bound capture (not required for this change).

## Not covered

- DSC01279 also has semantic masks, so its first visit may still wait on Vision. Not measured separately.
- The XMP is still decoded in full on every load. If a long history still costs time in the future, that decode is the next place to look.
- `readEditHistory` and the restore/validation paths now benefit from the record reuse, but were not timed.

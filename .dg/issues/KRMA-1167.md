## Objective

Give the analysis and mask disposable caches an application-level budget and make per-asset deletion cheap.

## Context

Source: audit `.context/audits/2026-11-03-code-quality-audit.md` (against `b7e46d5c`); probe source and outputs are in `.context/audits/evidence/2026-11-03/README.md`. Line numbers below are from that commit and may drift.

`PhotoAnalysisCache` and `MaskStore` write per-key files with no byte, age, or entry policy. Source, provider, and analysis-version changes leave old variants. A 60-megapixel Float32 mask alone is about 240 MB before transient copies. `PhotoAnalysisCache.remove` (`Models/PhotoAnalysis/PhotoAnalysisCache.swift:163`) and `MaskStore.remove` (`Models/PhotoAnalysis/MaskStore.swift:117`) each enumerate the whole cache and decode JSON to find one asset's records; deleting many photos repeats this per asset. Legacy mask metadata can contain an inline float array, making the "metadata" scan expensive. Code-confirmed; no measurements.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Entries are organized per asset or covered by a rebuildable ownership index so a batch delete is one bounded traversal.
- [ ] Byte and age caps with background cleanup of stale-version and orphan entries, yielding to visible work. Never evict saved recipes or canonical originals.
- [ ] Large binary reads are bounded before allocation; corrupt legacy entries and interrupted two-file writes are handled without crashing or leaking.
- [ ] Tests (operation-count based): deleting a large selection does not decode every record per asset; cap enforcement; stale version cleanup; corrupt/legacy entry.
- [ ] Record disk plateau and cleanup latency in the ticket for a repeated export-quality masking session across many photos.

## Implementation notes

- Do not change mask precision or quantize without a quality comparison; that is out of scope here.
